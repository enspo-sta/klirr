Guide

How Klirr works, and how far to trust it

Klirr predicts the distortion a loudspeaker driver adds when its voice coil moves far enough for its large-signal parameters to change: force factor Bl(x), suspension stiffness Kms(x) and inductance Le(x), plus the Doppler shift of a moving cone, the compressed air in the box and the jet losses in a port. It integrates the full nonlinear differential equations in time, so harmonics, intermodulation between any number of tones, the dynamic DC shift of the voice coil and compression all come out of one model instead of from separate rules of thumb.

The model

This is Klippel's lumped-parameter model of an electrodynamic driver (Loudspeaker Nonlinearities: Causes, Parameters, Symptoms), extended with an enclosure:

u = Re·i + d(Le(x)·i)/dt + uL2 + Bl(x)·v L2 branch: uL2 = R2(x)·(i − i2), d(L2(x)·i2)/dt = uL2 Mms·dv/dt = Bl(x)·i − Rms·v − Kms(x)·x + ½·i²·dLe/dx + ½·i2²·dL2/dx + Sd·p_box p_box = P0·[((V0 − z)/(V0 + Sd·x + Sp·xp + Sr·xr))^γ − 1] leak: dz/dt = p_box / R_leak port: Map·dvp/dt = Sp·p_box − Rp·vp − ½·ρ·ξ·Sp·vp·|vp|·s(|vp|) radiator: Mr·dvr/dt = Sr·p_box − Rr·vr − Kr(xr)·xr p(t at 1 m) = ρ/(Ω·r)·[Sd·a(t + x/c) + Sp·ap + Sr·ar]
  • Reluctance force ½·i²·dLe/dx pulls the coil toward higher inductance, as in Klippel's model. L2(x) and R2(x) follow the shape of Le(x), the same assumption Klippel uses for the X_L limit (Assessment of Voice Coil Peak Displacement Xmax).
  • Box air is compressed adiabatically (adiabatic process); leakage is a flow resistance set from Ql.
  • Port nonlinearity follows Vanderkooy's jet model: a loss ½·ρ·v·|v| that sets in above a critical air speed, with critical speed times port diameter held constant (5 m/s for a 75 mm port) and a smooth switch s(|v|) (J. Vanderkooy, Nonlinearities in Loudspeaker Ports, AES 104th Convention, 1998, preprint 4748, listed on the Waterloo Audio Research Group publication page). The Klippel tutorial describes the same effect: port Q above 50 at low levels, 10 or less above 20 m/s.
  • Doppler: the far-field pressure is the cone acceleration re-timed by x/c, the first-order solution of the moving-source delay (Beers and Belar, 1943; Lebrun and Hélie, 2020). It is applied to the cone only.
  • Radiation is an ideal point source in half space (2π) or full space (4π) at 1 m on axis. Mms is taken to include the air load, as datasheets do.
  • Filters are analog prototypes turned into biquads with the pre-warped bilinear transform (Audio EQ Cookbook); Linkwitz-Riley sections are squared Butterworth sections (Linkwitz Lab).
  • Numerics: classic fourth-order Runge–Kutta at 96 kHz or more (raised automatically for small inductances), inputs sampled exactly at the half steps, 8-point Lagrange interpolation for the Doppler re-timing, and coherent sampling so every tone and product falls on an exact FFT bin. A steady-state check compares consecutive analysis windows before a result is accepted.

Measured curves are fitted with a Chebyshev polynomial (order 10 by default) inside the measured range and continued with their end slopes outside it (Bl may only fall, stiffness may only rise). The shape of each curve comes from the large-signal data; the values at rest come from the small-signal table, so the linear response matches the datasheet. For the suspension you can switch a way to the large-signal Kms(0) in Setups. LSI reports recommend their "Large + Cold" values for system modelling (see the example report under Sources), but against the measurements below the small-signal table matched better, so it is the default.

What it leaves out

  • Current-dependent inductance Le(i) and flux modulation Bl(i), eddy-current and magnetic hysteresis. In good motors these set the distortion floor in the midrange.
  • Cone break-up and material nonlinearity, surround resonances, and any frequency dependence of the suspension (creep).
  • Voice-coil heating over time. You can set a fixed temperature rise per way instead.
  • Directivity, baffle diffraction and the room. Levels are on-axis point-source values at 1 m.
  • Port turbulence noise, and passive crossover interaction: filters act on the voltage, as in an active system. Series resistance can be added per way.

So the predictions are the excursion-driven part of the distortion. That part dominates bass and lower-midrange harmonics at high level and the intermodulation that bass excursion imposes on everything else; above a few hundred hertz real drivers usually measure higher than this model.

Klippel's thresholds reproduced

Klippel chose his displacement-limit thresholds so that each nonlinearity alone gives about 10 % distortion in a two-tone test with f1 = fs, f2 = 8.5·fs and a 4:1 voltage ratio (Table IV of the Xmax paper; application note AN5). The same test in Klirr, on a free-air 8-inch driver at 5 mm peak excursion:

Nonlinearity at its thresholdKlippelKlirr
Bl = b0 + b2·x², Bl falls to 82 %d3 ≈ 10 %d3 = 10.2 %
Bl = b0 + b4·x⁴, Bl falls to 82 %d3 ≈ 10 %d3 = 9.8 %
Le = l0 + l1·x, impedance at f2 changes 10 %d2 ≈ 10 %d2 = 9.8 %
Cms = c0 + c2·x², compliance falls to 74 %THD ≈ 10 %7.2 %
Cms = c0 + c4·x⁴, compliance falls to 77 %THD ≈ 10 %12.1 %
Cms = c0 + c1·x, compliance falls to 78 %THD ≈ 10 %6.8 %

The motor cases agree to half a percentage point. The suspension cases depend on the driver's damping at resonance, which Klippel's fictitious driver does not specify. The Doppler check also settles a detail: Klippel's X_D = 90.5·d/fs comes from Beers and Belar and corresponds to the root-sum-square of the two sidebands, which is 3 dB below the arithmetic sum that IEC 60268 uses for d2.

Klippel fits its curves over a working range xp− < x < xp+, where the coil spends more than 99 % of the time, and draws them further, out to ±Xprot, the largest displacement the protection system allowed (Klippel's large-signal identification manual). It evaluates its limits only inside the working range and prints ">" with the range's shorter side when a threshold is not reached there. The plots draw that range in black; research/lsi/xp_extract.py measures it from the same images, each built-in driver stores it as its fit range, and Klirr evaluates its limits inside it the same way. A ">" value matches Klippel's within 0.1 mm on all thirteen. Beyond the fit range the curves rest on less than 1 % of the measurement, so the Distortion table marks a coil that goes there. Published X_D values use the large-signal resonance, which Klirr now does as well when a Kms(x) curve exists. These published values are stored in research/erin/limits.json, and tests/test_engine.js checks this table against them and against the engine.

Adding your own drivers

  • Klippel LSI report: paste the polynomial coefficients (b, k or c, l) in Drivers and set the measured range.
  • Plots only, for example HiFiCompass Premium or a datasheet: digitise Bl(x), Kms(x) and Le(x) with your pixel-extraction pipeline and paste the points as "x, value" lines.
  • Your driver database: import drivers.json. Small-signal parameters are completed from fs, Qts, Vas, Sd, Bl and Re where possible, and "hd-frequency" measurements become overlays.
  • Saved drivers and setups go to your account when Klirr runs inside Claude, so they follow you between phone and computer; otherwise they stay in this browser. Export them as JSON any time.

Conventions

  • SPL is at 1 m on axis in the chosen radiation space. "Voltage set by SPL at one frequency" matches how sweeps are measured: one voltage for the whole sweep. Your database convention is 94 dB at 500 Hz for mids and 100 Hz for bass.
  • Harmonics are relative to the fundamental. THD is the root-sum-square of H2 to H5.
  • d2 and d3 follow IEC 60268 as quoted by Klippel: sums of the two sideband magnitudes relative to the voice tone f2.
  • Multitone distortion is the power in all non-tone bins of a band relative to the tone power in that band, after Czerwinski, Voishvillo and colleagues.
  • Klippel displacement limits follow the definitions in the Xmax paper and IEC 62458: X_Bl and X_C use the smallest ratio within ±X; X_L uses the blocked impedance at 8.5·fs with L2 and R2 following Le(x).

When to distrust a result

  • The table in Distortion shows ⚠ when the coil left the measured range; the curve extension beyond it is a guess.
  • It shows † when the coil went past Klippel's fit range ±xp but stayed inside the plotted curves. There the curves rest on less than 1 % of the measurement; the Drivers view shades that band.
  • Above a few hundred hertz, or below −60 dB, expect real drivers to measure worse than the model.
  • A driver with an estimated or single-limit shape tells you about that shape, not about the driver.
  • What would change these conclusions: a Klippel LSI curve set that disagrees with the pixel extraction, a measurement in the same conditions that departs from the model in the bass, or large-signal Rms data that change damping at high level.
